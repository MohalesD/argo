Direction notes
  1. Editorial type scale. One serif headline, one standfirst, small letterspaced labels everywhere else. A single reading order per screen serves locality: the card title anchors each object, and every control sits inside that object's visual neighborhood.
  2. Ticket anatomy with a notch pair. The notch and dashed rule split each card into a title zone and a metadata stub, so metadata and its actions live together at the stub, next to what they describe. Locality-first, preserved across all four views.
  3. The status spine. Board status is encoded by presence and color on the left edge, and the spine is a real control: click it, or press M, and the card-local Move menu opens. Metadata is actionable, never inert. Gold appears only on in-rotation stacks, keeping gold scarce and reserved for accomplishment.
  4. The magnetic canvas. Placement is thinking, so the canvas has zero chrome. Cards snap softly to an 8 px grid, alignment hairlines appear against neighbors, and every settle offers Undo move. Arrow keys move cards with the same saved-position feedback, keeping input parity between pointer and keyboard.
  5. Motion as the craft signal. Feedback belongs to the point of change: the star bursts at the star, the moved flash lands on the card, drag alone earns a lifted shadow. Nothing decorative persists at rest, and all decorative motion is disabled under reduced-motion. Immediate visible feedback, never a silent update.
