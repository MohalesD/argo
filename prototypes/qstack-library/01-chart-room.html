Direction notes
  1. Double neatline and a functional grid. The canvas carries an inner hairline neatline and a separate outer border with coordinate ticks, the two-border convention of real charts. The grid behind it sits at 5 percent alpha and earns its place: while a card is being dragged, the nearest grid lines light up as alignment guides, then fall silent at rest. Serves honest data: decoration must do work, and feedback belongs to the active state.
  2. Maritime document cards, flat at rest. White surface, hairline border, four corner tick marks like a plotting sheet, serif titles. Shadow appears only while dragging, so elevation always means motion, never decoration. Serves locality and honest state feedback: what you see on the card is the card's true state.
  3. The gold seal. A small matte seal, concentric rings around an eight-point star, appears only on accomplished stacks: 25 stars or more, or in rotation. It is the single gold ornament on a card face, so gold keeps its meaning. Serves gold scarcity: accomplishment is the only thing that earns the metal.
  4. Fork lineage lines in ink, never gold. Dashed curved lines on the canvas connect a parent stack to its fork, labeled "forked from" in letterspaced small caps, recomputed live as cards move. Lineage is fact, not praise. Serves honest data: relationships are drawn as record-keeping, not celebration.
  5. Waypoint labels and one corner compass rose. Cluster names render as small-caps tags with a compass tick glyph, placed like chart place names; empty canvas stays bare, and a single reduced-opacity compass rose sits in one corner as instrument linework. Every control stays on the card it affects, and the keyboard can do everything the pointer can: star, move, nudge, fan out. Serves locality and input parity.
