# Argo positioning and branding
Version 1.0, July 11, 2026. Author: Mohales Deis (mohalesdeis@gmail.com).

## Who this is for

Argo is built for a tribe, not a market. The target is a small group of
recruiters, talent acquisition experts, and people ops professionals who
try it and say: this is what I've been waiting for, it is a delight to
use, it has personality, I am hooked, I do not want to use anything else.

Three thousand people who feel Argo was built for them beats a million who
tolerate it. That is not a fallback position, it is the strategy.

## Who this is not for

Argo is not competing with Workday, Lever, or Loxo. It is not an
enterprise ATS and it is not trying to become one. Any analysis that
benchmarks Argo against companies that have raised nine figures is
answering a question Argo is not asking, and following that analysis will
make the product worse.

Enterprise is not the market. Early adopters and innovators are. Product
maturity gets built on top of a tribe that already loves the thing, not
ahead of one.

## Why personality is the product, not decoration

The category is crowded and beige. Taste and personality are the
differentiation, not a coat of paint over it. Design decisions that make
Argo feel like a specific thing made by specific people, with a point of
view, are load-bearing. A design rule that makes Argo more correct and
less distinctive is the wrong trade.

## The deeper reason Argo exists

Sales and marketing get rewarded. They get competitions, gongs,
leaderboards, and conventions the size of Dreamforce. HR gets none of it.
HR is one of the most disrespected functions inside most organizations,
and HR has to celebrate itself because nobody else will.

Argo is software that rewards HR. Recognition, craft, and status for the
people who do this work are not a feature, they are the reason the product
exists. Every mechanic that celebrates the practitioner, stars, seals,
earned marks, the marketplace, is downstream of this.

## What this means for the marketplace

The marketplace is a revenue channel, but a small and deliberate one. It
exists to be fun and to represent what Argo stands for, not to be the
breadwinner. Stacks made inside a company are freely shareable within that
company. The marketplace is where premium stacks live: from well-known
companies, and from the recruiters and CHROs the profession already looks
up to.

Content will leak. People will screenshot it and repost it. That has
always been true of anything worth having, and Argo is not designed to be
locked down. It is designed to be a pleasure to use.
