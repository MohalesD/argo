## 1. Position Statement

For job interviewers who need an easy, repeatable way to ask and document
interview questions and responses, Argo is a web-based platform that
gives quick access to role- and level-specific interview questions while
letting the interviewer log responses and assessments in real time.
Unlike printouts, spreadsheets, or ad hoc notes, Argo gives interviewers a
systematic way to reduce bias, run a consistent process, compare
assessments across candidates and fellow interviewers, and store
everything centrally for easy sharing.

The Argonauts framing lives in the business model and positioning doc,
not here, per your call.

## 2. Core Value Hypothesis

If Argo gives Overworked People Ops Managers and Ambitious External
Recruiters a complete interviewing solution with targeted questions
pulled from a verified bank, not generated fresh and hoped-for, they will
try it, customize it, use it more than twice a week, share it with
colleagues, and see reduced interview time and increased structure as a
result.

**Current alternatives.** A shared Google Doc of questions copied from
blogs, printed templates, a notepad, an abandoned Google Forms attempt
that fell apart once she needed a different form per role and level, and
as of 2026, an open tab to ChatGPT or Claude for a quick, unverified

**Empathy map, condensed:**
- **Thinks:** the current process will not survive the hiring wave. Risk
  of illegal questions, inconsistent evaluation, and losing good
  candidates to a bad process.
- **Sees:** peers at larger companies have ATS or HRIS (human resources
  information system) budgets she doesn't have. Her own hacked-together
  Google Forms solution is falling apart, and the company may move off
  Google Suite entirely.
- **Feels:** genuinely loves the work and the company, but carries real
  fear of a bad hire, a compliance mistake, or simply not keeping pace.
  Feels alone in owning this.
- **Does:** works roughly 55 hours a week. Interviewing already eats 20
  to 60% of her time before the hiring surge even starts.

**Jobs to be done:**
1. Get a reminder and ready questions before a scheduled interview
   without having to build them from scratch each time.
2. Assess candidates in real time during the interview, not from memory
   afterward.
3. Draw from a vetted question bank instead of improvising.
4. Enforce the same questions and standard across every interviewer for
   a given role.
5. Share responses and assessments with the team without manual chasing.
6. Trust that the question in front of her actually came from somewhere
   real, screened by people who know what a good interview question
   looks like, instead of just sounding like it did.

Job 6 is the whole argument for building Argo on retrieval (pulling from
a verified question set) instead of open generation (asking a model to
invent questions fresh every time). The 2019 docs already wanted this,
word for word: "a national database of best in class and most effective
questions." It wasn't buildable for real in 2019. It is now. This isn't
a pivot, it's finally building the thing that was already the plan.

### 3.3 New: Ian, Involved Functional Hiring Manager

Constructed July 5, 2026, as a working hypothesis, not pulled from real
interviews the way Olivia's material was. Treat as a first draft to test
against an actual human, not validated research.

**Snapshot.** Runs an engineering or sales team, 8 to 20 direct reports,
at a company past the earliest scramble stage but still small enough
that HR hasn't fully absorbed interviewing away from him. Interviews four
to eight candidates a month, more when actively backfilling. Didn't sign
up to become good at interviewing. Signed up to build a team, and
interviewing is the toll he pays to get there.

**Working hypothesis on his pain.** Not Olivia's problem wearing a
different job title. Olivia worries about consistency across the whole
company and about liability. Ian worries about one thing: can this
person actually do the job, and is he asking the right question to find
out before he's stuck managing someone who can't. He's likely the person
most tempted to skip whatever Olivia builds and wing it, trusting his own
gut over a shared question bank HR put together, which is exactly the
failure mode Olivia's own research already named: borderline
non-compliant questions, gotcha questions, interviewers showing up
distracted with nothing prepared.

**Screening question (working draft).** "How many candidates have you
personally interviewed in the last quarter, and did you write your own
questions or use ones someone else gave you?" The second half is doing
the real work, a proxy for whether he improvises by default or would
take structure if it were fast enough not to feel like a tax.

**Working job to be done.** As a hiring manager, I want a short set of
role-specific questions I trust are good, handed to me right before the
interview with almost no setup on my part, so I can spend my limited prep
time thinking about the candidate's background instead of writing
questions from scratch.

## 4. The 2026 AI Landscape: What Olivia's Alternatives Actually Look
Like Now

1. **Legacy, shrinking.** Printouts, spreadsheets, memory. Still real,
   not where the competitive pressure is anymore.
2. **Ad hoc AI, almost certainly the center of mass for the current
   beachhead.** Opens ChatGPT or Claude directly, asks for interview
   questions, gets something fluent back, uses it once, repeats from
   scratch next time. No memory across interviews, no verification, no
   way to know if it's biased or redundant. This is the alternative Argo
   is actually up against for most of Olivia and most of Ian, and it's
   more dangerous than a Google Doc ever was because it doesn't look
   unfinished.
3. **Automated but still ungoverned.** A more advanced user wires this
   through Cowork or a scheduled task, syncing to their calendar so
   questions generate automatically before a meeting, no manual prompting
   needed. Same underlying problem as tier 2, friction removed. Tells you
   the market is already halfway to wanting what Argo does, they just
   don't have anything trustworthy running underneath the automation yet.
4. **Fully autonomous, explicitly not a target right now.** Voice agents
   (tools like OpenClaw, with a voice MCP, Model Context Protocol,
   handling the actual call) running entire phone screens end to end, no
   human interviewer present. Real, observed, deliberately left alone for
   two independent reasons. Jordan's reason: several adoption stages past
   the current beachhead, building for a buyer who doesn't exist yet at
   volume means underbuilding for the one asking ChatGPT for questions
   right now. Nadia's reason, separate and sufficient on its own: once a
   voice agent's output materially influences a real hiring decision with
   no human in the loop, that's automated decision-making territory under
   GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation) Article 22, and employment AI
   sits in the EU AI Act's high-risk category under Annex III. Logged as
   a watched signal, not a target, both reasons attached on purpose.

## 5. Problem, Alternatives, and Value Proposition

| Top problems | Current alternatives customers actually use | Argo's value proposition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Not knowing what questions to ask for a given role and level | Googling questions, a shared cloud doc, gut feeling, an ATS with limited functionality, or asking ChatGPT directly and getting something fluent with zero way to verify it's actually good | A verified, role- and level-specific question set pulled from a real bank, not generated fresh and hoped for, available in minutes |
| No repeatable, objective structure across interviewers | Printed templates, informal team norms, memos that get ignored, or everyone independently prompting their own AI tool slightly differently | A locked, shareable interview flow every interviewer on a role follows the same way, with the same verified source behind it |
| No central place to capture and compare responses and assessments | Printouts that don't come back, an abandoned Google Form, email chasing, one-off AI chat sessions nobody saves | Real-time response capture and a simple, consistent rating scale, shared automatically |
